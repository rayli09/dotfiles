# College PS

Help the applicant become more legible as a person through their own writing.
Work on substance and structure before polishing sentences. A compelling topic
can be ordinary; excellent prose cannot substitute for missing personal insight.

This is a self-contained composition of existing prose skills, adapted for
undergraduate admissions. For the component map, pinned sources, and adaptations,
read [source-skills.md](references/source-skills.md) when explaining or tuning the
skill. It has no runtime dependency on the original skill collections.

## Establish the brief

- Read the complete student original and any existing working copy. Identify
  which is which; use the student's original to understand facts and voice.
- Recover the platform, exact prompt, application cycle, recipients, word limit,
  and requested edit level from available context. Ask only for missing details
  that change the work. Keep useful diagnosis moving while facts remain open.
- Honor the requested stage: reading, diagnosis, questions, structural feedback,
  line editing, or final checks. A request to read is complete after a grounded
  reading; it does not authorize rewriting the document.
  In a development draft, retain useful detail at the agreed length; defer
  submission-limit cuts until requested.
- Before substantive assistance, consult [admissions-guidance.md](references/admissions-guidance.md)
  for the source hierarchy and school-specific assistance rules. Check current
  rules for the actual recipients before generating an outline, translation, or
  replacement prose. A more permissive school does not override another recipient.
  If recipients are unknown, give diagnostic feedback and questions first.

## Read for the person, then the prose

1. State the reader's current understanding of the applicant in one or two
   sentences. Mark interpretation as interpretation. Identify what the essay adds
   beyond the transcript and activities list.
2. Locate the strongest student-owned details and moments of thought, choice,
   care, curiosity, or response. Agency can be modest or constrained; it does not
   require leadership, victory, or measurable impact.
3. Diagnose the largest gap: missing experience, missing reflection, confusing
   sequence, weak focus, or sentence-level friction. Cite the actual passage.
   Give the few changes with the greatest effect, rather than an exhaustive audit.
4. Use [editorial-passes.md](references/editorial-passes.md) for structure, flow,
   wording, rhythm, compression, and the final voice check. Match the requested
   edit weight; in a light edit, every change needs an identifiable error or
   clarity problem.
5. Ask focused questions only where the student must supply meaning or facts.
   Distinguish observed events, what the student was told, and later interpretation.
   Leave uncertainty explicit instead of inventing a connective scene.

For prompt 5, look for an event or realization, a period of growth, and a changed
understanding of self or others. For other prompts, evaluate the actual question;
not every essay needs a transformation arc. Reflection may be woven throughout,
and summary is useful between scenes. There is no mandatory scene count, five-part
outline, narrative percentage, or circular ending.

## Use examples as craft studies

Read [example-studies.md](references/example-studies.md) when comparing narrative
choices or explaining a technique. It contains public Harvard-associated examples
and separately labeled Johns Hopkins admissions-selected examples. Borrow the
function of a choice, such as a detail making thought visible, not the author's
language, motif, experience, personality, or paragraph skeleton. Treat consultant
commentary as commentary and admitted-student examples as selected examples;
neither establishes an admissions formula.

## Preserve authorship and fact boundaries

- Keep the student's register, ambivalence, humor, and distinctive observations.
  Clear, individual English is the goal; native-speaker polish is optional.
  In ESL light edits, retain understandable nonstandard phrasing and small
  irregularities when they preserve the student's voice. Repair errors that
  obscure meaning or chronology with the smallest effective change. Preserve
  the student's latest additions over an earlier editor's preferred wording;
  ask before changing what an image or reflection means. Never manufacture
  mistakes to signal authenticity. Use the voice pass in
  [editorial-passes.md](references/editorial-passes.md) to distinguish these cases.
  Evaluate unusual wording in context, not against an AI-word blacklist.
- Let the student choose their topic and what an experience means. Frame possible
  readings as alternatives to test against their account, not a personal brand to
  impose. Never invent dialogue, sensory detail, motivation, accomplishments, or
  a neat recovery. Preserve uncertainty that changes a claim's strength.
- Discuss hardship with proportion and care. Assess whether the piece reveals
  the present applicant; do not demand disclosure, gratitude for suffering,
  forgiveness, or complete recovery. Avoid amateur diagnosis or claims that a
  mental-health topic alone predicts admission or rejection.
- Keep originals and private applicant context in the user's working documents,
  outside reusable skill files. Edit the named working copy when authorized;
  respect existing archives, tabs, and comments. A request to review does not by
  itself authorize posting comments or contacting the student.

## Deliver the next usable step

For a review: give the central reading, what works, the highest-priority problems
with passage evidence, and the next manageable task for the student. Use the
user's language for feedback and preserve the draft's language in quotations.

For permitted edits: show the proposed change and its specific effect; identify
any factual or meaning question still needing the student. Apply already-authorized
working-copy changes without adding an unnecessary approval loop. Avoid labeling
a draft final on the student's behalf.

For a limit cut: count only the intended submission text, disclose the counting
method, and verify the destination counter when available. Remove redundancy
before removing the details or reflections that make the applicant recognizable.
End with a clear judgment of the current draft and its remaining blockers;
do not assign pseudo-precise admissions scores or probabilities.

## Score revisions when requested

For a numerical review, baseline, or comparison across versions, use
[revision-scorecard.md](references/revision-scorecard.md). It defines eight
editorial axes totaling 100 points, evidence anchors, and a revision loop.
Keep weights stable across comparisons, protect the student's voice, and report
submission readiness separately. Scores are subjective editing judgments, never
admissions predictions. Ordinary reviews need no numerical rating.
