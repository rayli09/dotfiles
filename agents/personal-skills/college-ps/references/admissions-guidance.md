# Admissions guidance and assistance rules

Sources checked 2026-10-24. Recheck prompt, word limit, and assistance policy for
the actual cycle and recipients when editing submission-bound work. Distinguish
published requirements from officer advice and this skill's editorial synthesis.

## What a PS does

- [Harvard Admissions: Application Tips](https://college.harvard.edu/guides/application-tips), Writing / Personal Essay: use the broad prompt to share something that matters to the applicant; an unusual subject is unnecessary. This is official guidance, not an admitted-essay formula.
- [Yale Admissions: Essays](https://admissions.yale.edu/essays): personal perspective matters more than an unusual subject. Feedback from people who know the student can help, while the student retains control and voice.
- [Yale Admissions: What Yale Looks For](https://admissions.yale.edu/what-yale-looks-for): writing supplies personal, qualitative insight within a wider academic and contextual review. Do not promise that prose can compensate for any academic profile.
- [Inside the Yale Admissions Office, Episode 4](https://admissions.yale.edu/posts/2020-06-15-episode-4-essays-what-works), 2020-06-15: admissions officers discuss voice, meaningful interests, vulnerability, reflection, and getting to know the applicant. They explicitly reject a perfect topic or formula. This is officer advice; the transcript itself distinguishes their views from university policy.
- [Episode 5](https://admissions.yale.edu/posts/2020-07-10-episode-5-essays-what-doesnt-work), 2020-07-10: common missed opportunities include excessive dramatic staging, narrative crowding out reflection, remaining in the distant past, and rehashing a resume. These are tendencies, not universal disqualifications or banned subject areas.

## Current prompt and length reference

- [Common App: 2026–2027 prompt announcement](https://www.commonapp.org/blog/announcing-2026-2027-common-app-essay-prompts/), published 2026-02-27: seven prompts, unchanged for this cycle. Prompt 5 asks about an event, accomplishment, or realization leading to growth and a new understanding of self or others. Choose based on the student's actual material; prompt popularity is not a quality measure.
- [University of Michigan: Essay Questions](https://admissions.umich.edu/apply/first-year-applicants/essay-questions): the Common App personal essay is 250–650 words; the upper limit is not a target. School supplements have their own requirements. Verify the actual input box for submission.

## Help allowed depends on the school

| Source | What the checked policy says | Operational consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Yale AI Policy](https://admissions.yale.edu/ai-policy) | Grammar/spelling review and general early-stage advice or topic suggestions are allowed; submitting substantive AI-generated application content violates its policy | Offer feedback and questions; keep substantive submitted expression with the applicant |
| [Cornell: Receiving Help](https://admissions.cornell.edu/policies/receiving-help-with-your-application) | Family feedback and grammar checks are acceptable; AI outlining, drafting, writing or translating responses, another person writing/rewriting, and someone else choosing the topic are listed as unacceptable | Avoid generating a new submission outline or replacement narrative for a Cornell-bound essay; explain problems in the existing draft and support the student's own decisions |
| [Michigan: Representing Your Authentic Voice](https://admissions.umich.edu/apply/first-year-applicants/essay-questions) | AI brainstorming, outlining, proofreading and organizing can help; application content must reflect the student's ideas and voice, with no copied AI-generated text | Assistance may include more structural support, while the applicant writes the submission |

Do not apply Cornell's rule as every college's rule, or Michigan's permission as
every college's permission. For an essay sent to several schools, account for all
actual recipients. An unknown policy is not permission. Keep critique and factual
questions moving while a requested form of substantive assistance is unresolved.
Do not convert an admissions policy issue into a blanket prohibition on helping.

## Editorial conclusions from these sources

These are this skill's working judgments, not quoted university rules:

- Judge the picture of the applicant produced by the draft, not the prestige of
  its subject or the sophistication of its adjectives.
- Distinguish an evocative image from an insight. An object can organize memory;
  the student's thinking must supply its significance.
- Let present understanding take space, without forcing hardship into a victory
  story. Ask for a changed perception or response when the student claims change.
- Use clear language and enough context for a reader unfamiliar with the student.
  A polished sentence that changes the facts or voice is a worse edit.
- Evaluate the PS alongside the rest of the application for duplication, while
  keeping supplemental fit questions distinct from the broad personal essay.
