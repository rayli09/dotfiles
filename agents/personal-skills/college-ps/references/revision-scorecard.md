# Revision scorecard v1

Use when the user asks for a numerical rating or wants to compare revisions.
This is an editorial rubric created for this skill, not a university rubric or
validated predictor. Rate the text, not the applicant's worth or admission odds.

## Fix the comparison conditions

Record the rubric version, exact draft/revision, prompt, review date, stage, and
working word count. Read the complete original for voice and fact context; score
only the named working draft. Store private scores and draft snapshots in the
applicant's workspace, outside this reusable skill.

Use the same weights and stage for a comparison. A development draft is judged
against its agreed scope; exceeding a later submission limit is a separate
readiness issue. A change of prompt, stage, or rubric requires an explicit new
baseline or a re-score of both texts. Do not retroactively rate an unavailable
earlier draft from memory.

## Axes: 100 points total

| Axis | Max | What this measures | Strong evidence | Typical limitation |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Person and agency | 15 | How legible this particular applicant is through attention, choices, responses, and values | The reader can describe the person beyond an adversity or activity label; modest or constrained choices count | Events happen around the narrator; actions are named without revealing their thinking |
| Reflection and emotional depth | 20 | What the student makes of the experience, including uncertainty and changed understanding where relevant | Interpretation grows from the experience and allows mixed feelings | A stock lesson, repeated claim of change, or an interpretation supplied by the editor |
| Narrative coherence and flow | 15 | Focus, chronology, causal or associative links, and development appropriate to the prompt | The reader can follow what happened and why each movement belongs | Confusing time shifts, a skipped turning point, or adjacent episodes with no legible connection |
| Specificity and supporting evidence | 15 | Whether selected observations, actions, or thoughts substantiate the claims | Details reveal something personal and do useful work | Generic community/activity language or assertions without support; extra description alone earns nothing |
| Student voice and ownership | 15 | Continuity with the student's own register, images, rhythms, and intended meaning | Individual expression survives editing and the student recognizes the account | A polished adult register, imposed insight, or interchangeable editorial phrasing |
| Language clarity and rhythm | 10 | Whether sentences convey the intended meaning without unnecessary decoding | Accurate references, understandable images, readable sentence movement | Ambiguity, misleading wording, or syntax that obscures meaning; clear ESL irregularities are not deductions |
| Opening and ending | 5 | Orientation and interest at the start; the contribution of the final beat | The opening admits the reader and the ending leaves an earned understanding, image, or question | An opening that withholds essential orientation or an ending that merely repeats an accomplishment |
| Focus and economy | 5 | Whether the space serves the person and purpose at the current stage | Detail and reflection earn their space; useful development material remains | Redundant explanations, repeated images/lessons, or disproportionate background |
| **Total** | **100** | | | |

Use these anchors within each axis: absent or seriously counterproductive
(about 0% of its maximum), weak (25%), developing (50%), effective with a visible
gap (75%), and strong throughout the relevant passages (100%). Choose whole-point
scores between anchors when the evidence warrants them; sum the awarded points.
These anchors support consistency, not measurement precision. Avoid decimals or
percentile claims. A one- or two-point total shift alone is inconclusive.

For each axis, report awarded/max points, a passage or paragraph reference, and
the specific remaining limitation. Assign a problem primarily to one axis;
deduct on another only for a distinct effect supported by the text. Do not let
one awkward sentence depress every category.

Voice is assessed from the student's available writing and confirmations, not
AI detection or a stereotype of ESL English. Do not reward prestigious settings,
the severity of suffering, elaborate vocabulary, flawless native idiom, a fixed
number of scenes, or a neat recovery. Agency can be noticing, reconsidering,
asking, persisting, or acting within limited choices.

## Keep readiness outside the total

Report unresolved factual/meaning questions and submission constraints
separately. Mark a score provisional when a material interpretation is still
unconfirmed. A resolved comment without an explanatory reply is not independent
verification of every claim. A high score cannot certify authorship, truth,
prompt fit, compliance with recipient assistance rules, or readiness to submit.

## Compare revisions without chasing the number

1. Establish the baseline with the table, a brief overall reading, and the
   highest-leverage one to three issues. Identify details and voice to preserve.
2. For the next pass, choose a small revision hypothesis: the exact reader
   problem, the passage involved, the smallest permitted change or student
   question, the primary axis, and what a better reading would make clear.
   Do not promise a numerical gain or require a new scene when existing material
   could solve the problem.
3. After an authorized edit, compare the actual before/after passages. Re-score
   changed axes using the same anchors; carry unchanged scores forward unless
   a demonstrated interaction or explicitly explained recalibration affects them.
   Report the total delta and any tradeoff. Keeping the same score is valid.
4. Accept improvement only when the reader problem improves without invented
   facts, changed meaning, or lost student voice. A higher total cannot offset
   these failures. Keep a clear student phrase when a polish-only alternative
   adds no useful meaning; do not keep revising solely to raise the total.

A compact review contains the baseline metadata, eight-row evidence table and
total, current readiness gaps, and the next revision hypothesis. A comparison
adds previous/current/delta columns and a short explanation of actual changes.
Do not fill the shared essay tab with score history unless the user requests it.
