# Public PS craft studies

Read 2026-10-24. These brief studies are original editorial analysis, not stored
copies of the essays. Follow the links for the texts. The three Harvard examples
are published as successful applications by a paid/sponsored Crimson feature;
they are not selected or endorsed by Harvard Admissions. Johns Hopkins is not an
Ivy League institution; its two examples are admissions-published with committee
comments. No example isolates the essay's causal effect on admission.

## Harvard-associated examples

### Claire — memory, poetry, and her grandfather

[Crimson Business, 2025 collection, Claire's Essay](https://business.thecrimson.com/10-successful-harvard-essays-2025).

An initially disconnected family memory is revisited through the student's own
poetry experience. The later performance gives the memory new meaning. Study how
the narrator's perspective changes; do not borrow the bereavement, imagery, or
lyrical ending. The adjacent consultant review names Clara and discusses a
different fish/immigration essay: its linkage is unreliable, so do not attribute
that commentary to Claire's text.

### Samantha C. — private imagination becomes shared work

[Crimson, 2023 collection, Samantha's Essay](https://www.thecrimson.com/topic/sponsored-successful-harvard-essays-2023/).

Childhood lists lead to online sharing, editing, and teaching. Accomplishments
support the same evolving relationship to writing. Study continuity of motivation
and enacted change. Do not infer that every PS needs a club, publication, or
community-service ending.

### Amy — The Color of Everything

[Crimson, 2023 collection, Amy's Essay](https://www.thecrimson.com/topic/sponsored-successful-harvard-essays-2023/).

An idea about perception becomes a way to explore learning and understanding
another person's work. Study conceptual flow and a concrete relational example.
It demonstrates that a PS need not center a crisis. Scientific claims in a model
essay still need checking; publication is not factual validation.

## Admissions-published comparison examples

### Ruoni T. — Clean Closet

[Johns Hopkins Admissions, Clean Closet](https://apply.jhu.edu/hopkins-insider/clean-closet/), class of 2030.

An ordinary sorting task develops into a changed relationship to memories and
new experiences. The committee highlights specific details and change through
action. Study how an object does work beyond decoration. The transferable question
is what changed in the writer's understanding; the closet motif and ending belong
to this writer. Its relevance to another object-centered draft is analytical,
not a reason to transplant the structure.

### Stella W. — The Art of Imperfection

[Johns Hopkins Admissions, The Art of Imperfection](https://apply.jhu.edu/hopkins-insider/the-art-of-imperfection/), 2024-08-08, class of 2028.

Difficulty drawing a hand leads to a different relationship with repeated effort,
later visible in unfamiliar coursework. Committee comments highlight applying
the understanding in another setting. Study the evidence supporting the reflection.
The later example need not be research, a prestigious course, or an achievement.

## Compare without imitation

For an example, identify the applicant impression, the actual textual support,
the function of the opening, the relationship between experience and reflection,
and what the ending changes. Separate your interpretation from published comments.
Then return to the student's own draft and ask which craft problem, if any, is
comparable. A technique is useful only when the student's material supports it.

Across this small selection, several choices are viable: revisiting a memory,
tracking a sustained interest, developing an idea, or demonstrating a changed
practice. None requires elevated diction, trauma, an exotic topic, or the same
paragraph plan. Do not imitate these writers' wordings or treat all their choices
as good just because the publisher reports an admission.
